Non-Negotiable

Safety Rules

Kabaragala is a friendly first mountain — but it's still a 1,500-m plateau in the inter-monsoon with ten people who've never done this before. These rules exist so we spend the trip enjoying the view.

  1. Buddy system alwaysNobody walks, photographs, or hits the toilet spot alone — pairs move as pairs
  2. Stay on the visible trailShortcuts through tea and pine slopes are how ankles and directions get lost
  3. No cliff-edge photosTwo metres back from any drop, photographer on the safe side
  4. Headcount at every breakRuwandika calls the pair letter, both names answer. Sixty seconds, every time
  5. Order of marchTheekshana sets the pace in front, Sanuja sweeps at the back — nobody ahead, nobody behind
  6. Three whistle blasts = STOPThe group holds where it is until the leads reach the whistler
  7. Phones on airplane modeOne phone per pair stays charged and reachable — power bank is for emergencies first
Weather & cold

Rain covers go on the moment tents are pitched. Heavy shower or lightning mid-hike: shelter in place 30 minutes, don't push on. Dinner eaten hot, everyone sleeps in their thermal layer. Abort rule: continuous heavy rain forecast on Friday morning = trip postpones. A lost weekend costs nothing; hypothermia practice costs plenty.

Leeches, blisters, water

Socks over trouser hems, soap-salt spray, check ankles at every break. Hot spot on your foot = stop immediately, Joseph tapes it — ten minutes early saves a limp on the descent. Drink even when not thirsty; one ORS sachet per person per day. Stream water: never untreated, full stop.
